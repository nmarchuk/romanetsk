# Welcome to Romanetsk

Romanetsk is a bustling metropolis where money comes first and life is cheap. Cosmopolitan and crowded, the city draws innumerable visitors from across the region.

Romanetsk was founded so long ago that nobody can remember who did it – but everyone knows why. The city has an accessible harbor, frequented by merchants as often as it is by pirates. Longshoremen unload barrels of cursed cod, moon carp, ember snappers, and crimson tuna onto the docks every day. Goods leave the city via a variety of trade routes than fan out into the vast steppes and mountain ranges beyond, and the deep Karsk river carries flat-bottomed riverboats loaded high with grains and mineral resources into the city.

The city is deeply stratified, both physically and socially. Wealthy aristocrats live in tall, ivy-covered buildings on the river’s edge with flower-covered balconies looking down at the busy docks below and at the endless sea beyond. An average person might find work in the many trading industries in the city, or in the inns and taverns serving hungry laborers and sailors at the water’s edge. The city’s poor live happy but cramped lives at or just below ground level, the poorest of them all living in basement rooms that occasionally flood during major storms.

Romanetsk once had walls surrounding the city. However, as it grew, these pesky stone structures got in the way of expansion, and citizens took it upon themselves to deconstruct these structures where a road may have been more convenient to build. After a horrible invasion nearly wiped out the city three hundred years ago, a strange and powerful warlock known as the Maker made a pact with a dark god to protect the city from invaders. He used this power to construct a terrible tower, the Eternal Watchtower, in the city center, and since then the city has been safe from invasion. In fact, this has been so successful that other cities have constructed their own Eternal Watchtowers.

The tower, made of black shining bricks and covered in thick vines, is so tall that it extends past the clouds. When the warlock died of old age, he left instructions for the keeping of the watchtower: The city shall choose three souls to work in service of the tower: the Watcher, the Keeper, and the Follower. What their function is a highly-guarded secret, but this much is known: the Watcher remains in the tower at all times. The Keeper stands guard over the Watcher and of the tower itself – they prevent anyone from entering and leaving. The Follower remains near the tower and trains to become the next Watcher, ready at a moment’s notice to become the next Watcher should the current one die or leave. The Watcher, Keeper, and Follower are paid handsomely for their service and for their secrecy.

With no need for a military, a thriving bureaucracy has sprung up in Romanetsk. The great wealth that flows through the city is organized, logged, and tax paid on it. Thievery and other petty crimes can be forgiven, but the primary concern of the Romanetsk government is that of tax evasion. Last week, it was found that the Watcher, Musaftar Khanakin, was delinquent on his taxes. When the Keeper was asked to deliver this message to him, he used the magic he had learned from his time as the Watcher to flee.

The Follower, Morse Doorblack, stood ready to take his place, but there’s long been rumors that he never truly wanted to be the Watcher, planning to flee across the sea long before Musaftar reached old age – now, he’s bound to a lifetime of living inside a cursed tower. There is no knowing if he will flee as well.

Your party is an elite squad of tax collectors, only a few months out of tax collecting school. You take pride in this work, shaking down arrogant merchants and hardened sea captains alike. The city officials have sent their most experienced tax collectors after Musaftar, and have not heard back. Desperate for answers, they have set you to the task: Find Musaftar, dead or alive, and bring him back to the Watchtower. Kill anyone that stands in your way, and do not, under any circumstances, tell anyone that he’s missing.
