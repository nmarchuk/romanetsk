# A History of Romanetsk, Years 1150-1175

*Most of the book contains boring details about the quality of the harvest each year, lists of people who were granted various titles and lands, marriages of nobles, and notes on the famous artists and philosophers of the day.*

On Mirtul 15, 1170, the Grand Vizier Adrian Bluecastle called the eight generals to bear arms against the foes of the realm of Romanetsk. Each brought an army of a thousand of their bravest men with them. The greatest and most learned Wizards were called down from their towers to stand in the realm’s defense. The largest and most fearsome beasts were chained and pulled down from the mountains to aid the realm in combat.

The Grand Vizier apportioned his forces in this manner: a thousand men, to guard the impenetrable fortress of Garetsk in the east. Four thousand men, to guard the vast farmlands and plains of Argovia–enough, the Vizier said, to guard each blade of grass. Two thousand men, to hold the strategic choke point north of Donetsk. The rest were left to defend the city of Romanetsk, half stationed in the vast harbor, and the other half on the thick city walls.

The Vizier was unable to predict that the invasion was to arrive by sea. The principal enemies of Romanetsk were Harthiel, a northern country too icy to host a proper harbor, and Krechek to the southeast, a landlocked nation. The Grand Viziers of Harthiel and Krechek formed an alliance. First, the Harthiel army amassed on the northern border of Romanetsk. They constructed siege weaponry, built camps, and even sent pigeons to be intercepted carrying false plans for a ground invasion. The Krechek army hired fast ships to sail around the Copper Islands and meet the Harthiel army. They boarded the ships in the cover of night, and set for Romanetsk.

The city was overrun with invaders before the Grand Vizier had time to react. Much of the city was burned to the ground, ransacked, and looted before dawn struck on the Summer Solstice. The forces amassed in the south were able to march north and drive off the invaders, but they had their victory–the humiliation of Romanetsk. A thousand years of history and architecture, burned, stolen, and eventually forgotten.

The Grand Vizier convened a Grand Council of archmages to come to a conclusion as to why this outcome was not foreseen or prevented by any of the greatest spellcasters in the land. The council discussed until the next festival day, and argued that the city of Romanetsk had outdated defenses; that the natural harbor made it easy to attack by sea; and that the forces of Romanetsk had been misallocated.

The Vizier had the Grand Council immediately dismissed. He called upon the Grandest Council, made up of those old archmages that had once been the teachers of the archmages that served on the first council. The archmages debated until two festivals had passed, and then came to the Grand Vizier with their answer: the greatest among them, the strongest and wisest mage in all the land, could have stopped the invasion, but did not. They reasoned that given his immense power, he should have been able to stop it.

This outcome satisfied the Grand Vizier. He immediately called the offending archmage to stand trial. The mage argued on the stand that the price of victory in that battle would have been far greater than that which Romanetsk paid by losing. Hearing this, the Grand Vizier sentenced him to death.

Yet, there was an uproar from the Grandest Council, who sat watching the trial. They demanded to know, before the mage was to be executed, what price the realm would have paid if they won. He refused to say, and offered himself freely to the guillotine. One archmage (history does not record which one) then intervened, casting a fireball that destroyed both the guillotine and the Grand Vizier himself. In the ensuing chaos, the Grandest Council was able to subdue the city guards, and gain control of the city. Thus ended the era of feudalism in Romanetsk.

The great archmage discussed his plan with the other archmages. He explained to the other mages that a dark god had reached out to him. The god knew that he valued the good of the realm above all else, and so offered to repel any invaders, in exchange for a price. The mage, knowing that such gods are tricksters, knew that this would be a false choice. However, the other archmages argued that the dark god already was punishing him for not agreeing–who else, to give the wretched Harthians favorable winds at sea? Who else, to give the feeble-minded Krecheks the idea to join forces with an ally a thousand miles away?

The great archmage agreed that a pact needed to be made, and soon. But he believed that there should be a way to keep the hand of evil at bay, even if this pact was accepted. So the mage went back to his books, studying the deepest and most arcane pieces of lore he could find. He conducted dangerous experiments on beasts and men alike. He was not seen again in public for ten years. For the sake of organization, the details of the mage’s plan are featured more prominently in the next installment, A History of Romanetsk, Years 1175-2000.

# A History of Romanetsk, Years 1200-1225

*Most of the book contains boring details about the quality of the harvest each year, lists of people who served on various councils, marriages of rich families, and notes on the famous artists and philosophers of the day.*

In the early months of 1201, the fleet of Parthia sought to invade Romanetsk by sea. As they were miles from landfall, the winds suddenly changed. The seas raged, and the entire fleet was dashed against the great rocks south of the harbor.

In 1205, the Harthian army invaded Romanetsk, with the help of giants. Their Vizier devised a plan to travel through the mountains, with the help of giants, to surprise the people of Romanetsk. The army was hit by a terrible plague. Those that did not die fled back to Harthiel, carrying the plague with them. It is said the nation still has not recovered.

In 1210, an earthquake rocked the entire continent. The fault line tore a hole through the landscape, cleaving great cities in two. This great crack in the earth stopped a mile outside the city.

In 1222, the great archmage who constructed the Eternal Watchtower died, after ushering in an era of thirty years of peace in Romanetsk. Per his instructions, a young mage, Cadrek Doorblack, was assigned to wait outside the tower. When the archmage died, terrible black smoke began to come from the tower, and young Cadrek entered the tower. Soon after he entered, the smoke ceased. The archmage’s body was never recovered.
