# Tax Collector Salary

| Level | Living Stipend | Commission | Festival Bonus |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | 6 GP | 10% | 5% |
| 3 | 8 GP | 10% | 5% |
| 2 | 15 GP | 10% | 10% |
| 1 | 30 GP | 20% | 15% |

- **Living stipend** is paid every two weeks. In DND, a week is ten days.
- **Commission** is taken on all taxes collected.
- **Festival bonus** is paid on festival days, on all income collected since the last bonus.

Note that Tax Collectors still pay taxes on all earnings, and must file separate returns.

# What Counts as Income

- Wages, bonuses, tips, etc.
- Commissions for services performed
- Return on investment
- Profits from a business
- Sales of items to merchants worth more than 99 GP
- Treasure
- Gifts in excess of 500 GP
- Inheritances — subject to an additional tax of 10% of all value
- The full value of any stolen goods, whether or not prosecution occurred

### Acts of Gods and Curses

The full cost incurred by an act of a deity may be deducted. The taxpayer must not have acted in a manner that begets divine intervention, including summoning that deity. A separate form must be delivered to the Hall of Ledgers explaining the circumstances.

The same form covers curses. Applicants may be granted a yearly tax credit equal to the monetary value of their curse, at the discretion of the Hall of Ledgers.

### Depreciation

| Item | Method |
| --- | --- |
| Magic items, by use | The item depreciates from its initial value via the square of the fraction of use the item has remaining. |
| Items that wear out | Depreciated over seven years via the straight-line depreciation method. |
| Ageless items | Refer to the 20-year MACRS depreciation table. |

# Retirement

Non-undead citizens who have reached 80% of their expected lifespan and no longer work, or who are disabled and unable to work, are not subject to ordinary income tax.

| Situation | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Continuing to live after death | 5,000 GP deduction |
| Becoming a Lich | A 5,000 GP tax penalty instead, in addition to taxes on necromancy |
| Tax debt after death | Carried over from one's mortal life |
| Resurrection after a year dead | May apply to have their tax debt forgiven |
| Joining a hive mind | May elect to be part of the hive mind's filing, or continue filing as an individual |

# Penalties

Failing to pay taxes will result in these consequences:

- A strongly worded letter, delivered by pigeon.
- A strongly worded letter, delivered by hand.
- A friendly visit from a tax collector.
- An unfriendly visit from a tax collector.
- Asset forfeiture.
- Arrest.

Interest on unpaid taxes accrues at an interest rate that varies based on the cost of money. This year, the interest rate has been set at 3%.

# Residency

Generally, all creatures who earn income within the realm of Romanetsk are subject to its taxes. The Special Administrative Region of Gatlinburg has a separate income tax regime, but participates on taxes on goods and services. Creatures who earn income on a separate plane but still reside on the mortal plane at least 51% of the time are considered to owe taxes on the mortal plane. In the past, this rule was more strict, but jurisdictional issues with the Feywild have caused the scope to be narrowed. Send a pigeon to the Hall of Ledgers if you find this update confusing.

# Filing

The best way to file taxes is by pigeon. Post offices are available in most major settlements and the fee is waived for the filing of taxes. The tax year ends when the year does (after five festivals) and taxes are due on the day of the first festival after the new year.

Magic users can apply to be a part of the Magical Energetic Tax and Excise Recovery System, or METERS. Generally magic users find this system to be more compatibel with their lifestyle. To apply, fill out the appropriate form and send a pigeon to the Hall of Ledgers with 10 GP and the form attached.

### Festival-based estimated Taxes

To prevent a large sum of money being owed at the end of the year, all businesses and citizens with income over 10000 GP/year are required to file estimated taxes on the day of each festival. If estimated taxes are not filed, and a citizen has a taxable income of more than 10000 GP, a penalty of 1% of the money owed will be assessed.
